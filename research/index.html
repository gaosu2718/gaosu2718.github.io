In-context operator learning for stochastic mean-field control

With Mo Zhou and Rongjie Lai

Stochastic mean-field control coordinates large populations of interacting agents under uncertainty. Existing numerical and deep-learning methods solve one problem instance at a time and must be re-optimized whenever the task changes. We instead treat stochastic mean-field control as an operator-learning problem: learn the map from a task to its solution, once.

The main obstacle is the diffusion term in the controlled Fokker–Planck equation, which rules out deterministic transport maps. Our model, the Normalizing Flow Invertible Solution Transformer (NFIST), combines the probability-flow ODE with an invertible normalizing-flow-based transformer. This recasts the dynamics as a deterministic continuity equation and gives the score in closed form, through the exact inverse and log-determinant of the flow, at 𝒪(d) cost per particle.

  • Mesh-free and self-supervised: trained end-to-end on the stochastic control objective, with no precomputed numerical solutions.
  • In-context learning: a task prompt, given as compact distribution parameters or as a raw particle cloud, conditions the transport map, so one pretrained operator solves unseen tasks in a single forward pass.
  • The operator-learning formulation is proven consistent with task-by-task optimization.
  • Zero-shot results on stochastic optimal control, Schrödinger bridges, systemic-risk control and obstacle-avoiding path planning.

Neural operators for spacecraft-swarm trajectory planning

With Sidhdharth D. Sikka, Zehui Lu, Rongjie Lai and Shaoshuai Mou

Satellite constellations need orbital transfers that save fuel and avoid collisions. Classical trajectory optimization scales poorly here, with both the number of spacecraft and the number of obstacles, because every pair needs a safety constraint.

We train a permutation-equivariant neural operator that maps the initial states and targets of a swarm, together with the obstacles, to collision-free, fuel-conserving trajectories. A batched Gauss–Newton step then enforces the exact orbital dynamics and reduces fuel use further.

  • Self-supervised: trained without optimal trajectory labels.
  • Trained on 10 spacecraft, it generalizes zero-shot to swarms of 1,000 spacecraft and 11,000 obstacles.
  • Matches the accuracy of a per-agent optimal control solver while keeping collision avoidance.

Analysis on the 2-nacci fractal

The Fibonacci word fractal is drawn from the Fibonacci words, each the concatenation of the two before it (fn = fn−1fn−2), by a turning rule with a turning angle α. I worked on a variation, the 2-nacci word fractal, built from the words fn = fn−1fn−1fn−2. It is also the attractor of an iterated function system of six similarities with ratio 1/c; at the critical angle α = arccos(1/3), c = 3.

That system overlaps itself (it fails the open set condition), so with Benjamin Steinhurst I built the fractal instead as a projective limit of graphs, and showed that the limit is homeomorphic to the attractor. Its finitely ramified cell structure supports a Laplacian: we assembled the Laplacian matrices of four graph approximations, computed their eigenvalues and eigenvectors, and estimated the spectral dimension from the eigenvalue counting function.

I presented the projective-limit construction at MAA MathFest 2019, and the analysis at the critical angle in a poster at the Joint Mathematics Meetings in 2022.

    Self-supervised In-context Operator Learning for Stochastic Mean-Field Control

    Suyi Gao, Mo Zhou, Rongjie Lai

    arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.18282, 2026

    arXiv PDF

    Stochastic mean-field control (MFC) provides a fundamental framework for coordinating large populations of interacting agents under uncertainty, with a wide range of applications. Existing numerical and deep-learning methods solve one MFC problem instance at a time and must be re-optimized whenever the task changes. In this work, we formulate stochastic MFC as an operator-learning problem and develop, to the best of our knowledge, the first mesh-free, self-supervised neural operator for stochastic MFC. The main challenge is that the diffusion term in the controlled Fokker–Planck equation precludes deterministic transport-map representations. We address this challenge by combining the probability-flow ODE with an invertible normalizing-flow-based transformer, which recasts the dynamics as a deterministic continuity equation and enables closed-form score evaluation through the exact inverse and analytical log-determinant of the normalizing flow, with 𝒪(d) cost per particle for networks of fixed size. Through transformer-based in-context learning, task prompts, represented by compact distribution parameters or raw particle clouds, condition the transport map, enabling a single pretrained operator to solve unseen tasks in one forward pass. The resulting Normalizing Flow Invertible Solution Transformer (NFIST) is trained end-to-end by minimizing the stochastic control objective directly, requiring no precomputed numerical solutions for training. We further prove the consistency of the proposed operator-learning formulation with task-by-task optimization. Numerical experiments on stochastic optimal control, Schrödinger bridge, systemic-risk control, and obstacle-avoiding path planning demonstrate effective zero-shot generalization while substantially reducing the computational cost of solving large families of stochastic MFC problems.

    Neural Operator Learning for Collision-Aware Trajectory Planning of Spacecraft Swarms

    Sidhdharth D. Sikka, Suyi Gao, Zehui Lu, Rongjie Lai, Shaoshuai Mou

    arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.00320, 2026

    arXiv PDF

    Satellite constellations require orbital transfers that are both fuel efficient and collision avoidant. Yet, the computational cost of optimization methods traditionally used to plan their trajectories scales poorly with both the number of satellites as well as the number of obstacles to avoid, due to the pairwise safety constraints. In this work, we introduce a permutation-equivariant neural operator for trajectory planning of spacecraft swarms. This neural operator maps distributions of spacecraft initial states, target states, and obstacle initial states to trajectories which avoid collision and conserve fuel. This neural operator output is then paired with a batched Gauss-Newton finish to enforce exact orbital dynamics, and further reduce fuel use. The operator is self-supervised, trained without optimal trajectory labels. When trained on ten spacecraft, the proposed method generalized zero-shot to swarms of 1,000 spacecraft and 11,000 obstacles. The generated trajectories matched a per-agent optimal control solver's accuracy while retaining collision avoidance. Operator learning grounded in physics may offer a fast, scalable alternative to trajectory optimization in the increasingly crowded orbits of the future.
